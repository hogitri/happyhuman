# Trademark check: Happy Human

> **This is research, not legal advice.** Register searches were done by an AI agent on 2026-10-26 using public tools. Some registers could not be reached. Confirm with Patentstyret or a trademark attorney (varemerkefullmektig) before filing or investing in stock with the logo.

## Question
Can "Happy Human" (name plus the H monogram logo) likely be registered and used as a trademark in Norway for an athleisure clothing line and fitness services? What should the founders do next?

## Summary
- **No "Happy Human" mark was found in the Norwegian register.** A text search of Patentstyret's register for "happy human", "happy humans" and "happyhuman" returned no hits on 2026-10-26. That register covers Norwegian national marks and international (Madrid) registrations that designate Norway. EU trade marks do **not** cover Norway.
- **The founders already have a head start.** The company **HAPPY HUMAN AS** (org.nr. 933037495, Øygarden, founded 2024-01-31, purpose "Undervisning innen idrett og rekreasjon") is registered with Kaja Haugen as daglig leder and Hoang Minh Trinh as styreleder. `happyhuman.no` is registered (since 2023-08-17) and redirects to the bootcamp's Facebook group. A business name gives some rights, but much weaker ones than a registered trademark.
- **The main risks are weak distinctiveness and the crowded "HAPPY" field, not an identical earlier mark.** This is my own judgement. "Happy Human" is a positive, laudatory phrase. For wellbeing services (classes 41 and 44), Patentstyret could object that it only describes the promised result. For clothing (class 25) it is more likely to pass. Many "HAPPY …" marks co-exist in class 25 in Norway (HAPPY SOCKS, FALKE HAPPY, PLAY HAPPY, Happy Hikers), which suggests "happy" alone is weak. That cuts both ways: the founders can probably register, but can't stop others from using "happy" or "human".
- **HUMANRACE and HUMAN MADE are low risk in Norway.** Neither was found in the Norwegian register by text search, and both share only the common word "human".
- **Other prior uses to know about:** the US word mark HAPPY HUMAN (class 41, personal training, Minneapolis), the humanist "Happy Human" symbol (Humanists UK; Norway's Human-Etisk Forbund made a 2005 version), and an Inderøy company named PRESENT, HEALTHY, HAPPY HUMAN AS.
- **Cost to file in Norway:** NOK 3 800 for the first class plus NOK 1 000 for each extra class. A word mark in four classes (25, 35, 41, 44) costs **NOK 6 800**. Filing the logo separately costs the same again.
- **Next step:** book Patentstyret's free guidance call, then file a word mark (with the logo filed too) through the company, ideally after a short attorney check. Details under [Recommendation](#recommendation).

### 3. Similar names and likelihood of confusion
**What the law says** (varemerkeloven, Lovdata):
- **§ 14 (distinctiveness):** a mark "må ha særpreg som kjennetegn for slike varer eller tjenester som det gjelder". It can't be registered if it consists "utelukkende" of descriptive indications. Patentstyret's Q&A gives examples of descriptive words that can't be registered: VINTER or WARM on a jacket, SUPER (quality), FAMILY (target group).
- **§ 16 (relative grounds):** a mark can't be registered without consent if using it would infringe someone else's right in Norway to "et varemerke, foretaksnavn eller annet forretningskjennetegn". So earlier **business names** count, not only trademarks.
- **§ 4 (scope of the right):** the exclusive right covers signs "identisk med eller ligner varemerket for varer eller tjenester av samme eller lignende slag".
- **§ 3:** rights arise by registration, or without registration once a mark is *innarbeidet* (well established through use). The bootcamp's current use is very unlikely to count as innarbeidet.
- **Patentstyret's test for confusion:** "a large part of the customer base would believe that two trademarks have the same commercial origin." Patentstyret checks distinctiveness and conflicts with earlier registrations when it examines an application. Third parties can file an opposition within 3 months after registration is announced.

**My assessment** (judgement, not from a source):

| Earlier sign | Likelihood of confusion with "Happy Human" | Why |
|---|---|---|
| HUMANRACE (Pharrell × adidas) | Low | Not found in the Norwegian register. "Human" is a common word. Different structure and different overall impression. |
| HUMAN MADE (Japan) | Low | Same reasons. Not found in Norway by text search. |
| HAPPY SOCKS and other "HAPPY …" marks in class 25 | Low to moderate | Many "HAPPY" marks co-exist in class 25, so "happy" alone is weak. "Happy Human" as a whole looks and sounds different from them. Happy Socks is a strong, well-known brand, so watch for objections if the clothing leans on bright, playful "happy" branding. |
| HEAL healthy body, happy mind | Low to moderate | Same classes and wellbeing message, but HEAL is the dominant element. |
| "Human …" marks in class 41 (Human Machinery, HUMAN PERFORMANCE CENTER, HUMAN BALANCE) | Low | Different first word, and "human" is common. |
| PRESENT, HEALTHY, HAPPY HUMAN AS (company, Inderøy, founded 2022: complementary treatment, teaching, music) | Low to moderate for 41/44 | It is an earlier business name containing "Happy Human", and § 16 covers business names. It works in a nearby field, but its name as a whole is longer and different. Worth an attorney's view. |
| US HAPPY HUMAN (class 41) | None in Norway; high in the US | Trademarks are territorial |
| Humanist "Happy Human" symbol | Low legal risk for a word mark or the H monogram | The risk is mainly reputational or associative. Keep the logo from looking like a stick figure with raised arms. |

**Is "Happy Human" descriptive or weak?** (judgement)
- **Clothing (25) and retail (35):** "Happy Human" doesn't describe clothes, so it should have enough distinctiveness. It is suggestive rather than descriptive.
- **Fitness and wellbeing (41, 44):** this is the real risk. An examiner could see "Happy Human" as a laudatory slogan that only promises the result of the service (you become a happier person). Marks like HEAL healthy body, happy mind and Human Machinery did get registered in 41/44, but the first is a combined (logo) mark and the second is a less obvious phrase. **Not verified:** whether Patentstyret has refused similar "happy + noun" word marks for wellbeing services. No case was found. The rejected international registration "happy summer" (class 28) gave no reason in the register.
- **Practical effect:** even if it is registered, protection is "thin". The founders can stop "Happy Human" and close copies, but not others using "happy" or "human" in their own names.
- **Mitigation:** file the **logo (H monogram + wordmark) as a combined mark** as well as the word mark. A combined mark is easier to get through, though it protects less. If the word mark is refused in 41/44, the combined mark still gives some protection.

### 5. Process, cost and timeline in Norway
**How to apply** (Patentstyret):
- Apply digitally through **Altinn** ("Start a trademark application"). The applicant should be **HAPPY HUMAN AS**, so the company owns the brand.
- Use **pre-approved terms** from the product selector and **pay when you submit**. This gives a first response in about **3 weeks**; otherwise expect a response within 4 months of payment.
- Patentstyret's statistics page (last 12 months, undated) shows an average of **4.0 months to the first substantive letter** and **5.7 months to registration** across all applications.
- After registration, others have **3 months to file an opposition**.
- Registration lasts **10 years from the application date** and can be renewed indefinitely.
- You should start **using** the mark for the registered goods and services within **5 years**; otherwise the rights can be lost for the parts that aren't used.
- **Priority:** a Norwegian filing gives **6 months** to file abroad (EU, Madrid) with the same priority date.
- Patentstyret offers a **free guidance call with a trademark expert** (+47 22 38 73 00, Mon–Fri 09:00–15:45). It also mentions paid preliminary searches. The current price wasn't found; a 2003 notice said NOK 900/hour, which is outdated.

**Fees** (Patentstyret price page, last modified 2026-09-25):

| Item | NOK |
|---|---|
| Application incl. registration, 1 class | 3 800 |
| Each extra class | 1 000 |
| Renewal (every 10 years), 1 class / each extra class | 3 400 / 1 300 |
| Opposition against someone else's mark | free |
| Clearance fee for an international (Madrid) application through Patentstyret | 800 |

**Worked examples:**
- Word mark "HAPPY HUMAN" in classes 25 + 35 + 41: **NOK 5 800**.
- Same with class 44 added: **NOK 6 800**.
- Word mark plus a separate combined logo mark in the same 4 classes: **NOK 13 600**, before any attorney fees.

**EU and international, later:**
- An **EU trade mark** covers EU member states but **not Norway** (Patentstyret Q&A). EUIPO's online fee is about **EUR 850** for 1 class, **+50** for the 2nd and **+150** for each class from the 3rd. These figures are confirmed by several secondary sources, not by the EUIPO fee page itself.
- **Madrid Protocol:** use the Norwegian application as the base and designate countries or the EU. WIPO's basic fee is **CHF 653** (black and white) or **CHF 903** (colour), plus a fee per designated country or region (complementary fee CHF 100, or an individual fee for some members; the EU individual fee wasn't verified). Patentstyret also charges its NOK 800 clearance fee.
- **When it makes sense:** only when there are concrete plans to sell clothing outside Norway, for example Shopify shipping to Sweden or Denmark. If that's likely within **6 months of the Norwegian filing**, file within the priority window. Run a proper EU search first; TMview couldn't be checked here.
- **The US is a separate problem:** HAPPY HUMAN is already registered there for fitness services.

## Options compared

| Option | Pros | Cons | Fit for Happy Human |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Word mark "HAPPY HUMAN" only** (25, 35, 41 ±44) | Broadest protection: covers any font or logo. Cheapest (NOK 5 800–6 800). | Highest risk of a § 14 objection in 41/44 | Good core filing |
| **B. Combined mark (H monogram + "Happy Human")** | Easier to get registered. Protects the chosen logo. | Narrower: protects that look, not the name in other forms | Good complement, and a fallback if A is refused |
| **C. Figurative H monogram alone** | Protects the chest-logo use on clothing | Simple letter marks need a distinctive design. Hasn't been searched against other H logos. | Consider later, once the logo is final and as a vector |
| **D. A + B together** (recommended) | Best coverage in Norway. Lowers the risk that the whole brand is left unprotected. | Double the fees (≈ NOK 13 600 for 4 classes) | Best balance before investing in clothing stock |
| **E. Wait / don't register** | No cost now | Others could file first. Weak position against copycats. Shopify, Instagram and marketplaces need a registration for brand-protection tools. | Not recommended before launching the clothing line |
| **F. EU or Madrid at once** | Wider coverage | Higher cost. EU not searched. Not needed for a Norway-only start. | Later, within the 6-month priority window if exports are planned |

## Recommendation
*Research, not legal advice.* In order:

1. **Confirm the logo** (H monogram plus wordmark; see [[Features/Brand Identity]]) and get it as a vector file. The combined-mark application needs the final artwork.
2. **Confirm assets are owned by the company.** Check that the `happyhuman.no` holder is HAPPY HUMAN AS (look it up at Domeneshop or Norid) and that @happyhuman.no is the founders' account.
3. **Book Patentstyret's free guidance call** (+47 22 38 73 00). Ask how they view "Happy Human" for classes 41/44 (descriptive or laudatory?) and how they view the earlier company name PRESENT, HEALTHY, HAPPY HUMAN AS. Ask for a price for a preliminary search that includes a **figurative search for the H monogram**.
4. **Talk to a varemerkefullmektig (trademark attorney) before filing** if any of these apply: Patentstyret signals a descriptiveness problem; the founders plan to order clothing stock or spend real money on the brand; or EU or other exports are planned within 6 months. A short clearance opinion typically costs a few thousand NOK. *Not verified:* no current price list was found, so ask for a quote.
5. **File in Norway through Altinn, with HAPPY HUMAN AS as applicant:** word mark "HAPPY HUMAN" and the combined logo mark in **25, 35 and 41**. Add **44** only if health or nutrition counselling will really be offered. Use pre-approved terms and pay on submission to get the ~3-week response.
6. **Note the filing date in the calendar.** The 6-month priority deadline for an EU trade mark or Madrid filing runs from that date. Also note renewal 10 years after filing and the 5-year use requirement.
7. **Until registration:** use "Happy Human" consistently. Avoid using ™ in ways that suggest a registration, and only use ® after registration. Don't design clothing graphics that echo the humanist "Happy Human" figure or Happy Socks' style.
8. **Later:** before shipping outside Norway, run an EUIPO/TMview search and decide between an EU trade mark and a Madrid designation. Treat the US as off-limits for fitness services under this name unless an attorney advises otherwise.

This should become a decision note once the founders choose what to file (e.g. `Decisions/… Register Happy Human trademark`); the main agent or the user records it.
