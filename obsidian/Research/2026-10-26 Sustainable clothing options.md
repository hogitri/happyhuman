# Sustainable clothing options and legal green claims

## Question
What concrete, realistic options does Happy Human have to make its clothing line genuinely sustainable, and to make sustainability claims that are legal in Norway? Covers sourcing models, materials and certifications, Norwegian and EU green-claims rules, practical measures beyond materials, and a phased plan with example wording.

## Summary
- **Start with certified blanks plus decoration, not print-on-demand and not custom production.** Buy organic cotton blanks from a brand with real third-party proof (Stanley/Stella or Neutral are the strongest verified options). Get them embroidered or screen-printed in small batches, sold as pre-order "drops". This gives quality control and low MOQs. You can also back up specific claims about the material. Print-on-demand is the cheapest way to test designs, but it's the weakest on quality and on a "sustainable" story. Custom production only makes sense later, once there's proven demand.
- **Make specific claims only. Never make a general one.** "Bærekraftig", "miljøvennlig", "grønn" and "klimanøytral" on their own are already risky under Forbrukertilsynet's practice. Under the new rules they'll be outright banned unless the product has an official ecolabel. Write what's true and checkable, for example: "Laget av 100 % økologisk bomull, sertifisert etter GOTS". Don't put "sustainable resources" into customer-facing copy as it stands. Keep it as an internal goal.
- **The law is changing, and the Norwegian date isn't set.** Stortinget passed the Norwegian version of EU Directive 2024/825 ("empowering consumers for the green transition") on 11 June 2026, and the law has been sanctioned. The government said on 19 August 2026 that the start date has **not been set and will be first half of 2027 at the earliest**. In the EU (e.g. Denmark) the same rules have applied since **27 September 2026**, which matters if Happy Human sells to Swedish or Danish customers. Some Norwegian industry articles say 27 September 2026 also applies in Norway. That's wrong, according to the government's own update. The separate EU **Green Claims Directive** is stalled and not adopted.
- **The most credible "sustainable" story for a small brand** is to make fewer garments that last longer: durable fabric, made to order or in small drops (no overstock), simple plastic-free packaging, a repair or take-back offer, and honest, specific product pages.

## Findings

### 1. Sourcing models

**a) Print-on-demand (POD) integrated with Shopify**
- Gelato says it has no minimum orders and no leftover stock, with 140+ production partners in 32 countries including Norway. Which products are made locally depends on the product ([Gelato Norway page](https://www.gelato.com/en-US/print-on-demand/norway), [Gelato products](https://www.gelato.com/products)). I couldn't verify which apparel items or blank brands Gelato produces *in Norway*.
- Printful's European facilities are in Riga (Latvia) and Barcelona (Spain) ([Printful support](https://support.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/41396775395729-Where-are-Printful-products-printed)). Printful offers Stanley/Stella blanks, but its page only confirms printing them in the US ([Printful Stanley/Stella](https://www.printful.com/stanley-stella)).
- Teemill (UK) markets itself as "eco print on demand" with organic products designed to be sent back and remade. It's free to install with a per-order commission. Reviews on the Shopify App Store mention slow shipping and slow support, and one reviewer says returns go back to the merchant ([Teemill on Shopify](https://apps.shopify.com/teemill)). I found nothing specific on shipping to Norway.
- *My assessment:* POD avoids overstock, which is a real sustainability benefit. Its weak points for Happy Human are these:
  - You can't control the quality of each garment, which undercuts the "exceptional quality" USP.
  - Embroidery and tone-on-tone looks are limited.
  - Unit costs are high.
  - Each order ships on its own from abroad. From the EU or UK into Norway, that also raises customs and VAT questions, which I didn't research here.
  - The printer usually isn't GOTS-certified, so the finished product can't carry the GOTS label (see section 2).
  - Most competitors use the same blanks, so POD alone isn't a distinctive "sustainable" story.

**b) Certified blanks plus decoration (embroidery or screen print)**
- **Stanley/Stella** (Belgium):
  - Certifications: GOTS for organic cotton and GRS for recycled fibres (both via Control Union, licence CU 819434), plus OEKO-TEX.
  - Fair Wear member with "Leader" status since 2021. Fair Wear itself says membership is not a product or factory certification.
  - Publishes sustainability and social reports and Fair Wear Brand Performance Checks ([Stanley/Stella certifications](https://stanleystella.com/en-eu/sustainability/certifications)).
  - Says its garments are made for all decoration methods: screen print, DTF, DTG and embroidery ([Stanley/Stella decoration](https://stanleystella.com/en-de/about-stanley-stella/decoration-like-no-other)).
  - Sold through distributors and decorators (a "Find a partner" page). I didn't find a confirmed Norwegian distributor. BI's Norwegian brand store sells an embroidered Stanley/Stella crewneck, so the route works in Norway ([BI brandstore](https://www.brandstore.bi.no/products/bi-brodert-crewneck-navy)).
- **Neutral** (Copenhagen, Denmark; production in India):
  - B2B producer of certified apparel. Claims GOTS, Fairtrade, EU Ecolabel, SA8000 and OEKO-TEX ([neutral.com](https://www.neutral.com/)).
  - Several Neutral tees, sweatshirts and hoodies are listed in the EU Ecolabel database under licence DK/016/049 ([EU Ecolabel product example](https://environmental-data.ec.europa.eu/ecolabel/products/product_53638.html), [Svanen listing](https://www.svanen.se/en/product-types/textiles-eu-ecolabel/ladies-sweatshirt-100-organic-fairtrade-cotton-o83001-eu-gb-ch)).
  - Some products are cotton "in conversion" (organic farming during the transition years), not fully certified organic.
  - Example price: a German B2B reseller lists a Neutral organic tee at about EUR 9.11 net for 1 unit, cheaper from 12 and 100 units ([Clubmartin](https://www.clubmartin.de/en/Organic-Cotton-T-Shirt-155-g-m2/NE60002)). Prices change. Treat this as indicative only.
- **Native Spirit** (via Toptex): made in Portugal and sold as blanks. Some items are GOTS "in conversion", in packs of 50 ([Toptex](https://www.toptex.com/ns314ic-eco-friendly-unisex-t-shirt.html)). Not checked in depth.
- **Local decoration (Bergen):** a directory search finds candidates, but I couldn't confirm their capabilities, minimums or certification. Examples: Norstrøm-Berggreen Broderi (Fjøsangerveien 197, embroidery; [Brønnøysund](https://virksomhet.brreg.no/en/oppslag/enheter/929185080)), Yes Sport og Profil AS (Nesttun) and others on [Gulesider](https://www.gulesider.no/brodering/bedrifter). Ask them directly about minimums, thread and ink types, and GOTS certification.
- **GOTS and decoration:** GOTS requires every processing step to be certified. In 2019 a German court (OLG Stuttgart) ruled for GOTS against a printer that printed GOTS blanks without being certified and sold them as GOTS products. Explaining GOTS on the printer's website didn't help ([GOTS press release](https://gotslive.global-standard.org/images/press/gots_press_release___gots_obtains_court_decision_against_textile_printer.docx), [Ecotextile](https://www.ecotextile.com/?p=36573)). **So with a non-certified local decorator, Happy Human can't put the GOTS label on the finished product.** It can describe the fabric factually, but should check the exact wording with the blank supplier or GOTS (see open questions). A GOTS-certified decorator exists in Germany (Shirt-King, GOTS-certified digital printing and an official Stanley/Stella dealer; [shirt-king.de](https://www.shirt-king.de/eng)). I didn't find a Norwegian one.
- *My assessment:* this is the best fit for launch. MOQs are low (blanks from 1 piece through resellers; decorators often have small minimums, to be confirmed). You can check every garment before it ships. Shipping blanks in bulk to Norway and sending from Bergen is likely lower in transport emissions than single-parcel POD from abroad, but I haven't measured that. And the material claims are backed by third-party certificates.

**c) Small-batch custom production (own pattern, fabric and labels)**
- Portugal is the usual European option for small brands. Vendor and blog sources give MOQs of about 50–100 pieces per style or colour at some factories, and 250–300 pieces for fully custom garments through brokers ([Thygesen Apparel blog](https://thygesenapparel.com/blog/top-activewear-manufacturers-in-portugal-trusted-partners-for-quality-sportswear)). These are marketing figures, not verified quotes.
- *My assessment:* this gives the most control over fit, fabric weight, colour (sage, powder blue) and quality, and it's the most distinctive. It also needs the most capital: MOQ times colours times sizes, plus samples and travel or audits. And it carries the overstock risk the brand wants to avoid. If the factory isn't certified, you need your own documentation (e.g. GOTS transaction certificates for the fabric). Worth doing for 1–2 hero products once drops sell out.

### 2. Materials and certifications: what each one actually proves
| Mark | What it proves | What it doesn't prove | Notes for Happy Human |
|------|---------------|-----------------------|-----------------------|
| **GOTS** | Organic fibre content (label grade "organic" ≥95%, "made with organic" ≥70%), plus environmental and social criteria at every certified processing step ([GOTS label grades](https://global-standard.org/certification-and-labelling/labelling/label-grades)) | That the whole garment is "sustainable" or better for the climate overall | The label on the final product needs a certified chain, including the decorator. Licence number and certifier must be shown. "100% organic" isn't a valid label grade ([GOTS logo use guide](https://gotslive.global-standard.org/images/events/Using_the_GOTS_Logo_on_and_off_product.pdf)). Old sources give a fee of about EUR 120 per facility per year and a turnover threshold; current figures not verified. |
| **OCS / GRS** | Share of organic (OCS) or recycled (GRS) content; GRS also covers some processing criteria | Overall environmental benefit | Stanley/Stella holds GRS. In the Zalando case, content certificates alone weren't enough to support "sustainable" claims (see section 3). |
| **OEKO-TEX Standard 100** | The product was tested for harmful substances (human safety) | Environmental or social performance. OEKO-TEX itself says it's not an eco claim. "Made in Green" is a separate label ([Hohenstein FAQ](https://www.hohenstein.de/en-us/oeko-tex/output-control/standard-100/faq)) | Fine to state as "tested for harmful substances". Never present it as a green label. |
| **Fair Wear Foundation** | The brand is a member working on labour conditions in its supply chain, with yearly Brand Performance Checks | Product or factory certification (Stanley/Stella's page says so) | Happy Human is far too small to join: eligibility is a minimum annual turnover of about EUR 2.5m ([Fair Wear membership](https://www.fairwear.org/about/membership/), not re-checked today). You can say a supplier is a member, factually. |
| **Fairtrade (cotton)** | Fair prices and premiums for cotton farmers | Conditions at the sewing factory | Available on some Neutral items. |
| **bluesign** | Chemical and input management across the supply chain. "bluesign APPROVED" covers inputs; "bluesign PRODUCT" covers finished goods ([bluesign criteria](https://www.bluesign.com/en/criteria)) | Organic or recycled content | Mostly relevant for synthetic or technical athleisure (leggings, performance tees), less for cotton basics. |
| **EU Ecolabel** | Official ISO 14024 type I ecolabel. Under Directive 2024/825 it's one of the few ways to show "recognised excellent environmental performance", which is what a general green claim needs ([Danish and Swedish preparatory material summarised by Miljömärkning Sverige](https://regeringen.se/contentassets/13ef70e74acc4c0d8f4f064aae104d52/miljomarkning-sverige-ab.pdf)) | n/a | Some Neutral blanks hold it. **Unverified:** whether a decorated, rebranded Neutral garment sold as Happy Human can still carry it. Probably not without Happy Human's own licence. Ask Neutral or the licensing body. |
| **Nordic Swan Ecolabel (Svanemerket)** | Official Nordic type I ecolabel. Textile criteria cover fibres (organic, recycled or lower-impact), chemicals across production, ILO labour conventions, and quality and durability in version 5 ([Svanemerket background doc v5](https://svanemerket.no/content/uploads/2021/08/039do_5_0_BD.pdf), [2020 consultation](https://svanemerket.no/content/uploads/2021/08/039_Brev_horing_tekstiler_2020_NO.pdf)) | n/a | Has a two-part licence (brand owner plus producer). The current criteria run until 31 Dec 2026, so a revision is likely. No textile fee figures found. Very few apparel blanks carry it. Realistic only as a later goal with a custom producer. |

Materials, in my judgement:
- For tees, long-sleeves and hoodies, **certified organic cotton** (GOTS) is the practical, well-documented choice.
- **Recycled cotton or recycled polyester blends** (GRS) suit fleece and hoodies.
- Recycled polyester sheds microfibres and is hard to recycle again, so don't present it as a full solution.
- Heavier fabric weights generally last longer, but that has to be shown by testing, not assumed.

### 3. Rules for green claims in Norway

**The law today: markedsføringsloven plus Forbrukertilsynet's guidance**
- Forbrukertilsynet's guidance on sustainability claims ("Veiledning om bruk av bærekraftpåstander i markedsføring") is version 2, last modified August 2020. It applies markedsføringsloven, which implements the Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC ([PDF](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Veiledning-om-bruk-av-bk-p%C3%A5stander_versj2.pdf)). I couldn't extract the full PDF text, so the points below come from secondary summaries.
- Claims must be documentable, correct and clear. Vague claims like "grønn", "bærekraftig" and "etisk" shouldn't be used without a concrete explanation. Minor improvements must not be overstated. A claim without adequate documentation is normally treated as misleading and unlawful. Label criteria must be disclosed and met ([CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/nor/publication/cms-green-globe/norway/norge-paastander-om-baerekraft-og-groennvasking)).
- **Clothing is a focus area.** In February 2020 Forbrukertilsynet wrote to 53 clothing businesses about general claims and unexplained labels ([CMS](https://cms.law/en/nor/publication/cms-green-globe/norway/norge-paastander-om-baerekraft-og-groennvasking)).
- **Norrøna/Higg (2022):** Forbrukertilsynet found Norrøna's marketing of an organic cotton tee, based on generic Higg MSI data comparing it with conventional cotton, misleading. It warned H&M and the Sustainable Apparel Coalition ([just-style](https://www.just-style.com/news/hm-group-norrona-under-fire-in-norway-over-environmental-claims/), [Forbrukertilsynet letter to SAC](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/sac-misleading-marketing-of-organic-cotton-garments-using-higg-msi-data-liability-as-an-accessory.pdf)). **Lesson:** don't claim "X % less water or CO2 than conventional cotton" based on industry averages.
- **Zalando (2024):** a joint European case led by Forbrukertilsynet. Zalando had to remove misleading sustainability claims and icons across the EU/EEA by 15 April 2024. Certificates for recycled or organic *content* didn't prove a product was environmentally friendly *overall*; that needs documentation covering the whole lifecycle ([CMS](https://cms.law/no/nor/publikasjon/bruk-av-generelle-miljoepaastander-i-markedsfoering-av-fast-fashion)). **Lesson:** a "sustainable" badge on an organic tee is exactly what was struck down.
- Forbrukertilsynet has also advised against "klimanøytral" and "klimakompensert" claims ([search summary of Forbrukertilsynet material](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/22-797-orientering-om-lovkrav-ved-bruk-av-klima-og-miljopastander-i-markedsforing-av-strom-l1273609.pdf), a 2022 letter to the power sector).

**The new law: Directive (EU) 2024/825 in Norwegian law**
- **Status:** the government put forward Prop. 38 LS (2025–2026) on 20 March 2026. Stortinget passed the law changes at its meeting on 11 June 2026 (Lovvedtak 85 (2025–2026)). The law says it applies "fra den tid Kongen bestemmer" ([Stortinget lovvedtak](https://stortinget.no/no/saker-og-publikasjoner/vedtak/beslutninger/lovvedtak/2025-2026/vedtak-202526-085/)).
- **Start date:** the government's page, updated 19.08.2026, says: "Lovsaken er ferdigbehandlet og sanksjonert. Tidspunkt for ikrafttredelse er ennå ikke fastsatt. Ikrafttredelse vil tidligst bli første halvår 2027." The proposition also covers EEA Committee incorporation of the directive ([regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/arbeiderparti-regjeringen-foreslar-na-nye-regler-i-markedsforingsloven-som-skal-gjore-det-enklere-for-forbrukerne-a-ta-miljovennlige-valg/id3153423/)).
- **Conflicting sources:** NHO Service og Handel (May 2026) said the rules apply from 27 September 2026 ([NHO SH](https://www.nhosh.no/bransjer/handel2/nyheter/2026/nye-krav-til-gronn-markedsforing-kan-du-ikke-dokumentere-det-skriv-det-ikke/)). That's the **EU** date (e.g. Denmark, [Dansk Erhverv](https://www.danskerhverv.dk/presse-og-nyheder/nyheder/2026/marts/nye-regler-om-gron-markedsforing-i-2026-det-skal-du-vide/)). I trust the government's later update more: **no Norwegian date yet, H1 2027 at the earliest.** Check Lovdata before launch.
- **What it adds** (in markedsføringsloven, angrerettloven and avtaleloven):
  - New definitions: "miljøpåstand", "generell miljøpåstand", "bærekraftsmerke", "holdbarhet" ([Stortinget lovvedtak](https://stortinget.no/no/saker-og-publikasjoner/vedtak/beslutninger/lovvedtak/2025-2026/vedtak-202526-085/)).
  - 12 new practices that are always banned, among them ([Tenden Advokatfirma](https://tendenadvokat.no/aktuelt/nye-regler-som-styrker-forbrukervernet-i-det-gronne-skifte), [NHO SH](https://www.nhosh.no/bransjer/handel2/nyheter/2026/nye-krav-til-gronn-markedsforing-kan-du-ikke-dokumentere-det-skriv-det-ikke/)):
    - a **general environmental claim** without recognised excellent environmental performance (in practice: EU Ecolabel, an official ISO 14024 ecolabel such as Svanemerket, or a top class under EU law);
    - a **sustainability label** not based on a certification scheme or set by a public authority, so **no self-made "Happy Human Conscious" badge**;
    - presenting a legal requirement as a special feature of the offer;
    - claims about a whole product that only apply to part of it, and neutrality claims based on offsetting (both from EU commentary, e.g. [Projet Celsius](https://projetcelsius.com/en/blog/banned-environmental-claims-eu/); the Norwegian wording isn't checked);
    - misleading claims about durability and repairability.
  - Claims about **future** environmental performance (e.g. "net zero by 2030") will need a concrete, independently checked plan.
  - New pre-contract information duties for online sales, including information on durability and the statutory complaint period, in a harmonised format.
  - Forbrukertilsynet enforces it. NHO says sanctions include fines of up to 4% of annual turnover.
- **Forbrukertilsynet's guidance:** the government says Forbrukertilsynet will update its guidance. I found no updated version as of today.

**The EU Green Claims Directive (separate)**
- The Commission announced on 20 June 2025 that it intended to withdraw the proposal, and trilogues stopped. It hasn't been formally withdrawn, and the Parliament lists it as "blocked" ([ESG News](https://esgnews.com/eu-commission-confirms-green-claims-directive-not-withdrawn/), [Linklaters](https://sustainablefutures.linklaters.com/post/102lrh5/eu-sustainability-initiatives-in-the-commission-2026-work-programme)). Norway's EEA notes say negotiations hadn't resumed as of 20 May 2026 ([EØS-notat, updated 24.05.2026](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/sub/eos-notatbasen/notatene/2023/mai/gronnvaskingsdirektivet/id2983987/)). **Not law, and no date.**

**Other rules coming that touch a clothing brand**
- **Extended producer responsibility for textiles in Norway:** Miljødirektoratet's draft (consultation 7 May – 21 Aug 2026) proposes that businesses selling clothing, textiles and shoes, explicitly including online sellers, join an approved producer responsibility organisation and pay for collecting and treating textile waste. The proposed start is **1 January 2027**. I found no threshold for small producers, and the regulation isn't final ([regjeringen.no](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/aktuelt/tekstilprodusenter-ma-belage-seg-pa-a-betale-for-tekstilavfall/id3158677/), [Miljødirektoratet hearing](https://www.miljodirektoratet.no/hoeringer/2026/mai-2026/horing-av-forslag-til-forskriftsregulering-av-utvidet-produsentansvar-for-tekstiler-og-handtering-av-tekstilavfall/)). This is likely to apply to Happy Human when it starts selling clothes.
- **EU ban on destroying unsold clothing (ESPR):** applies to large companies from 19 July 2026 and medium-sized ones from 2030. Small and micro companies are exempt ([Cooley](https://products.cooley.com/2026/05/07/deep-dive-eu-finalises-new-requirements-for-unsold-consumer-products-under-espr/)). Not relevant for Happy Human now; EEA status not checked.

### 4. Practical measures beyond materials
- **Production is where most of the climate impact is.** An EPRS briefing citing the EEA puts it at about 80% for textiles, with the use phase at about 14% ([EEA textiles](https://www.eea.europa.eu/publications/textiles-and-the-environment-the)). *Judgement:* making fewer, longer-lasting garments that actually get worn is the biggest lever a small brand has. Overstock is pure waste.
- **Durability as a claim:** "holdbar" becomes a defined term, and misleading durability claims are banned. If you want to say "built to last", test it: pilling, shrinkage after washing, colour fastness and seam strength, with a lab or the supplier's test reports. Then state specific results ("tested for 50 washes at 40°C" only if that was actually tested). Fabric weight and construction help, but don't prove anything on their own.
- **Pre-order drops and made to order:** sell each drop as a pre-order for 2–3 weeks, then order blanks plus decoration for confirmed sales plus a small buffer. This matches the startup budget and avoids unsold stock. Be clear about delivery time to meet angrerettloven and consumer expectations.
- **Packaging:** recycled or recyclable paper mailers, no polybag where the supplier allows, no unnecessary inserts. Claims must be specific ("emballasjen er laget av resirkulert papir"). Norway also has packaging producer responsibility rules; thresholds not checked here.
- **Shipping:** offer a pickup point by default. Offer free collection at bootcamp and meet-ups (this also fits [[Decisions/2026-10-26 Online store only]]). Avoid express air by default. Don't call a delivery option "klimavennlig" unless you can document it.
- **Repair and take-back:** a simple offer such as free embroidery repair, spare-thread advice, or "send back worn Happy Human garments and get a discount" with garments passed on for reuse. Name the partner and what happens to the garments. Don't promise "recycled into new clothes" unless that's actually documented.
- **Care guide:** wash cold, air dry, and how to care for embroidery. It extends garment life and is a safe, useful piece of content for the blog.

## Recommendation
*This section is my judgement, based on the findings above.*

**Phase 0: now, before any launch**
- Stop using "bærekraftig" or "sustainable resources" in public copy as a general description. Turn the USP into concrete commitments the team can document, e.g. "certified organic cotton, made in small batches, built to last".
- Request certificates and documents from the chosen blank supplier: GOTS scope certificate and licence number, transaction certificates per order, OEKO-TEX certificate, and its Fair Wear report. Store them in a shared folder; that folder is the documentation for every claim.
- Plan for textile producer responsibility (proposed from 1 Jan 2027).

**Phase 1: launch (first 1–3 drops)**
- Two or three products: tee, long-sleeve, hoodie, in 2–3 muted colours. Use Stanley/Stella or Neutral organic blanks with a small tone-on-tone embroidered chest logo. The motivating-message piece is screen-printed with water-based ink, if the decorator offers it.
- Sell through Shopify as **time-limited pre-orders**. Order blanks and decoration after the pre-order closes. Inspect and dispatch from Bergen. Offer pickup at meet-ups.
- Paper packaging, a care card, and a simple repair offer for the embroidery.
- Optional: use POD only for low-volume extras (e.g. a tote bag), and don't market those as sustainable.

**Phase 2: after proven demand (6–18 months)**
- Durability testing of the core products, then specific durability claims.
- A take-back or resale scheme with a named partner.
- Look for a GOTS-certified decorator (Nordic or German, e.g. Shirt-King) so the finished product can carry the GOTS label.
- Consider small-batch custom production for one hero hoodie in a signature colour. Ask the factory about GOTS, EU Ecolabel or Svanemerket certification.
- Only when a product actually holds the EU Ecolabel or Svanemerket in Happy Human's name can general claims like "miljømerket" be used for that product.

**Example wording that would be allowed** (if documented)
- "Laget av 100 % økologisk bomull (GOTS-sertifisert)." *(Name the certificate and keep the transaction certificate on file. Use the GOTS logo only if the whole chain, including the decorator, is certified.)* / "Made from 100% certified organic cotton."
- "Plagget er produsert av Stanley/Stella, som er medlem av Fair Wear og har status som Leader." *(A factual statement about the supplier; check the status yearly.)*
- "Testet for skadelige stoffer etter OEKO-TEX Standard 100." *(Safety, not environment.)*
- "Vi produserer bare det som er forhåndsbestilt, så vi sitter ikke igjen med usolgte klær." *(Only if true.)*
- "Emballasjen er laget av resirkulert papir og kan kastes i papiravfallet."
- "Ødelagt brodering? Send plagget til oss, så reparerer vi det gratis."
- "Et klesplagg du bruker lenge, er bedre for miljøet enn et nytt." *(A general statement about clothing, not a claim about the product. Low risk, but keep it modest.)*

**Wording to avoid**
- "Bærekraftig", "miljøvennlig", "grønn", "øko-vennlig", "ansvarlig", "bevisst", "naturvennlig", or "sustainable", "eco-friendly", "conscious" as standalone descriptions of products or the brand. After the new rules start, these are banned without an official ecolabel; today they're already risky.
- "Klimanøytral", "CO2-nøytral", "klimakompensert" or "netto null", especially if based on offsetting.
- "Bruker X % mindre vann/CO2 enn vanlig bomull" based on industry averages (the Norrøna and Higg lesson).
- A self-made badge or icon such as a leaf, a globe or "Happy Human Green Choice".
- "100 % økologisk" next to the GOTS logo, or the GOTS logo at all with a non-certified decorator.
- "Resirkulerbar" or "sirkulær" without a documented take-back route.
- "Laget for å vare livet ut" or "holder i mange år" without test documentation.
- Presenting things the law already requires (e.g. REACH chemical compliance, a 2-year complaint period) as special benefits.

**Should become a decision:** "Launch clothing with certified organic blanks plus decoration, sold as pre-order drops, and use specific claims only." The main agent or the founders should record that in `Decisions/` if they agree.
